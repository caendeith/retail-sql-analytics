## 5. Data caveats

- The source file ends on **2011-12-09**, so 2011-12 is a partial month. Its negative month-over-month growth in query 01 and its low totals in queries 03–05 are truncation artefacts, not real declines.
- The same truncation inflates 2011-12 in query 05: refunds processed in the first nine days of December are booked against only nine days of December sales, which is why that month's cancellation ratio spikes. Treat the final month of any of these series as incomplete.
- The catalogue contains non-product adjustment codes that behave like products — `POST` (postage), `M` (manual), `C2` (carriage), `D` (discount) and similar. They are genuine revenue lines in the source data, so they are kept, and they can legitimately rank inside a country's top 5 in query 04 (visible for Germany and EIRE). Filtering them out is a one-line change if a purely merchandise view is wanted.
- The file begins on **2010-12-01**, so 2010-12 is the first observable cohort/month and has no comparable prior period.
- RFM recency (query 02) is measured from the last invoice in the file, not from today, which keeps the segmentation reproducible.
- LTV (query 06) is observed 12-month value on a ~12-month file, so it is a floor on true lifetime value, not a forecast.
- A cancellation is recorded in the month the refund was processed, which may be later than the original sale; query 05's `lost_revenue_pct` is a monthly cancellation-intensity ratio rather than a matched reversal.
